Citation metrics are approximate as of **August 2026** and fluctuate daily.

## 1. Yoshua Bengio

**Title**: Full Professor & Founder, Mila – Quebec AI Institute
**University**: Université de Montréal, Québec, Canada
**Born**: March 5, 1964, Paris, France

| Metric | Value |

| **h-index** | ~259 |
| **i10-index** | ~700+ |
| **Turing Award** | 2018 (shared with Hinton & LeCun) |

### Landmark Contributions
- **Deep Learning Revolution**: Co-pioneer (with Hinton and LeCun) of deep learning — the approach that now powers virtually all modern AI systems including ChatGPT, AlphaFold, autonomous driving, and computer vision.
- **Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)**: His student Ian Goodfellow developed GANs under Bengio's supervision, creating one of the most influential generative AI architectures.
- **Attention Mechanisms & Sequence-to-Sequence Models**: Key contributions to attention-based neural networks that directly led to the Transformer architecture.
- **Representation Learning**: Foundational research on learning distributed representations of words and sentences, underpinning modern NLP.
- **AI Safety & Responsible AI**: Recent work focuses on the societal risks of AI, governance frameworks, and the control problem.
- **First Living Scientist to 1M Citations**: On October 27, 2025, Bengio became the first living scientist in any field to surpass 1 million citations on Google Scholar.

## 2. Geoffrey Hinton

**Title**: University Professor Emeritus
**University**: University of Toronto, Ontario, Canada
**Born**: December 6, 1947, London, England

| Metric | Value |
|:-------|------:|
| **Total Citations** | ~1,060,000+ |
| **h-index** | ~194 |
| **Turing Award** | 2018 (shared with Bengio & LeCun) |
| **Nobel Prize** | 2024 Physics (shared with John Hopfield) |

### Landmark Contributions
- **Backpropagation**: Co-author of the foundational 1986 paper that demonstrated how neural networks could learn via backpropagation of errors, making deep learning practical.
- **Boltzmann Machines & Restricted Boltzmann Machines**: Pioneered generative stochastic neural network architectures.
- **Deep Belief Networks**: Demonstrated in 2006 that deep networks could be trained layer-by-layer, reigniting the field of deep learning after two "AI Winters."
- **AlexNet / ImageNet Revolution**: Supervised Geoffrey Krizhevsky and Ilya Sutskever on AlexNet (2012), which won ImageNet by a massive margin and catalyzed the modern deep learning era.
- **Dropout Regularization**: Co-invented dropout, one of the most widely used techniques to prevent overfitting in neural networks.
- **Capsule Networks**: Proposed capsule networks as a more robust alternative to CNNs for recognizing spatial hierarchies.
- **AI Safety Advocacy**: Left Google in 2023 to speak freely about existential risks posed by advanced AI systems.

## 7. Leslie Lamport

**Title**: Distinguished Scientist (Retired January 2025)
**Organization**: Microsoft Research, Mountain View, California
**Born**: February 7, 1941, New York City, USA

| Metric | Value |
|:-------|------:|
| **Total Citations** | ~95,000+ |
| **h-index** | ~88 |
| **Turing Award** | 2013 |

### Landmark Contributions
- **Paxos Consensus Algorithm**: Invented the Paxos family of protocols for achieving consensus in unreliable distributed systems. Paxos and its variants (Multi-Paxos, Raft-inspired designs) are the foundation of every production distributed database and coordination service (ZooKeeper, etcd, Chubby, Spanner).
- **Lamport Timestamps & Logical Clocks**: Introduced the concept of logical clocks and the "happens-before" relation (1978), establishing the theoretical framework for reasoning about time and causality in distributed systems. This paper is arguably the single most important paper in distributed systems.
- **Byzantine Fault Tolerance**: Co-formulated the Byzantine Generals Problem (1982), establishing the foundational framework for reasoning about fault tolerance in the presence of arbitrary (malicious) failures — essential for blockchain, critical infrastructure, and aerospace systems.
- **TLA+ (Temporal Logic of Actions)**: Created TLA+, a formal specification language used by Amazon (AWS), Microsoft, Intel, and others to verify the correctness of complex distributed systems before implementation.
- **LaTeX**: Created LaTeX (1984), the document preparation system that is the global standard for scientific and mathematical typesetting, used by virtually every academic in STEM fields.
- **Sequential Consistency**: Defined the concept of sequential consistency in shared-memory multiprocessors, fundamental to modern concurrent programming.
- **Bakery Algorithm**: Invented the Bakery algorithm for mutual exclusion in concurrent systems without hardware support.

## 8. Christos Papadimitriou

**Title**: Donovan Family Professor of Computer Science
**University**: Columbia University, New York, USA
**Born**: August 16, 1949, Athens, Greece

| Metric | Value |
|:-------|------:|
| **Total Citations** | ~104,000+ |
| **h-index** | ~139 |

### Landmark Contributions
- **Computational Complexity Theory**: Authored the definitive textbook *Computational Complexity* (1994), used universally in graduate CS programs. His work establishing the complexity landscape of optimization problems (PPAD-completeness, the complexity of equilibria) is foundational.
- **Algorithmic Game Theory**: Pioneered the intersection of computer science and economics. Proved that computing Nash equilibria is PPAD-complete (2006), establishing that finding market equilibria is computationally intractable in general — a result with profound implications for economics, market design, and mechanism engineering.
- **Database Theory & Query Optimization**: Fundamental contributions to the theory of database query optimization, chase algorithms, and the complexity of conjunctive queries.
- **Approximation Algorithms**: Major contributions to the theory of approximation algorithms for NP-hard problems, including TSP and MAX-SAT.
- **Computational Neuroscience (Recent Work)**: Developing "Assembly Calculus" — formal computational models of how neural circuits in the brain give rise to cognition, language, and reasoning.
- **Author & Educator**: Co-authored *Algorithms* (with Dasgupta and Vazirani), one of the most widely used algorithms textbooks. Also wrote *Logicomix*, a graphic novel about the foundations of mathematics featuring Bertrand Russell.

## 9. David Patterson

**Title**: Professor Emeritus; Distinguished Engineer, Google
**University**: University of California, Berkeley
**Born**: November 16, 1947, Evergreen Park, Illinois, USA

| Metric | Value |
|:-------|------:|
| **Total Citations** | ~150,000+ |
| **h-index** | ~90 |
| **Turing Award** | 2017 (shared with John Hennessy) |

### Landmark Contributions
- **RISC (Reduced Instruction Set Computing)**: Co-invented the RISC processor architecture at Berkeley in the early 1980s. RISC principles now dominate the world: ARM processors (powering virtually all smartphones, Apple Silicon Macs), RISC-V (the open-source ISA Patterson champions), and MIPS all derive from RISC research.
- **RAID (Redundant Arrays of Inexpensive Disks)**: Co-invented RAID (1988), the storage architecture that uses multiple commodity disks for reliability and performance. Every enterprise storage system, NAS device, and cloud storage backend uses RAID concepts.
- **Computer Architecture Education**: Co-authored *Computer Organization and Design* and *Computer Architecture: A Quantitative Approach* (with John Hennessy), the two most widely used computer architecture textbooks globally — fundamental to every CS and EE education.
- **RISC-V**: A primary advocate and leader of the RISC-V open-source instruction set architecture, which is disrupting the global processor market by providing a free, open alternative to proprietary ISAs like x86 and ARM.
- **Networks of Workstations (NOW)**: Led the NOW project at Berkeley, demonstrating that clusters of commodity workstations could replace expensive supercomputers — a direct precursor to modern cloud computing architecture.
- **Domain-Specific Architectures**: Recent research focuses on TPUs and domain-specific accelerators (DSAs) for AI/ML workloads, arguing the end of general-purpose processor scaling.

## How These Rankings Are Measured

### Primary Metrics

| Metric | Description | Source |
|:-------|:-----------|:------|
| **h-index** | A researcher has h-index *h* if they have *h* papers each cited at least *h* times. Balances productivity and impact. | Google Scholar |
| **D-index** | Discipline-specific h-index filtering only CS publications and citations. More accurate for cross-disciplinary researchers. | Research.com |
| **Total Citations** | Raw count of how many times a researcher's papers have been cited. Indicates breadth of influence. | Google Scholar |
| **c-score** | Composite metric accounting for co-authorship, self-citations, and career vs. single-year impact. | Stanford/Elsevier Top 2% |
| **i10-index** | Number of papers with at least 10 citations. Measures sustained productivity. | Google Scholar |
